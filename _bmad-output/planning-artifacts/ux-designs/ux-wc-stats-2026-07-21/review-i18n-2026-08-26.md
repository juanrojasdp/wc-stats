# i18n & Terminology Review — 2026-08-26 delta

Scope: the story 3.7 / UX-DR24 delta only — the landing badge set, the nine nav
destinations, the L1465 ruled abbreviations, the `/players` position
abbreviations, the `lg` text-expansion assumption, and the register of all new
copy in `mockups/key-landing-mobile.html`, `mockups/key-navigation.html` and
`mockups/key-players-index-mobile.html`. Everything the prior `review-i18n.md`
closed is treated as closed and is not re-reported. Checked against the shipped
dictionaries `app/src/locales/es.ts` (3,040 lines) and `app/src/locales/en.ts`
(1,607 lines), which are the real precedent, not the spine's prose.

## Overall verdict

The register is right and the disclosure copy is genuinely well-matched to
shipped precedent, but the delta was written as prose and never carried into the
locale layer: it specifies **nineteen new user-facing strings and zero keys**,
appends **no rows to the per-term policy table**, and four of its nine
destination labels are a **second Spanish name for a surface that already ships
one** — most visibly *Tops* against `leaderboards.title` = "Líderes del torneo",
which the badge's own supporting line contradicts by saying "tablas de líderes".
Two rulings are affirmatively wrong rather than merely unspecified: `POR`
abbreviates *portero*, the exact word this spine rejected in favour of *arquero*,
and the en `viz.table.shirt` head "NO." substitutes a different word while the
tooltip still says "Shirt". The `lg` inline-nav assumption fails on the delta's
own arithmetic before any independent check.

## 1. The eight landing badge labels and their supporting lines

- **[critical]** `Tops` is an unnecessary anglicism *and* a second Spanish name for a surface that already ships one. `leaderboards.title` = **"Líderes del torneo"** / "Tournament leaders" (`es.ts:2253`, `en.ts:1230`) is the `<h1>` of the very page the badge points at, so a reader taps *Tops* and lands on *Líderes del torneo*. The Spanish-first tie-breaker ("translate unless no usable Spanish exists") is decided against "Tops" by the app itself: usable Spanish exists and is shipped. The badge is internally inconsistent on top of that — its own supporting line reads "36 **tablas de líderes**". The one shipped use of "top" is the fixed phrase `leaderboards.teaserOverflowMore` = "más en el **top 3**", where it is a naturalized quantity idiom, not a standalone plural noun label. The route slug `/tops` is unaffected: slugs are ruled language-neutral English, so this costs one string, not a route. (EXPERIENCE.md "The ruled badge set", row 4; `mockups/key-landing-mobile.html` badge 4; `mockups/key-navigation.html` frames A and C.) *Fix:* es **"Líderes"**, en **"Leaders"**. Supporting line es **"36 tablas del torneo, de equipos y de jugadores."**, en **"36 boards, team and player."**
- **[high]** `Acerca de` is a *third* name for `/about` and is a dangling preposition in Spanish. The app ships `chrome.footer.aboutLink` = "Acerca del sitio" / "About this site" (`es.ts:65`) and `about.title` = "Acerca del sitio" / "About this site" (`es.ts:76`) — one destination, one term, already ruled. "Acerca de" alone is a calque of the English "About": *acerca de* is a compound preposition that requires a complement, so standing alone it reads as a truncation rather than a label. The landing mock makes this concrete by printing "Acerca de" in the footer, where the shipped string is "Acerca del sitio". (EXPERIENCE.md badge row 8; `mockups/key-landing-mobile.html` badge 8 and `<footer>`.) *Fix:* es **"Acerca del sitio"**, en **"About this site"** — reuse `chrome.footer.aboutLink` rather than minting a badge twin. If the extra ~45 px is unaffordable in the inline nav, that is an argument for area 5's threshold change, not for a fourth name.
- **[high]** The landing mock replaces the ruled attribution footer with a different string carrying a **mistranslation of PMSR**. `chrome.footer.attribution` is ruled verbatim in this spine and ships as "Datos: informes oficiales post-partido de la FIFA — Copa Mundial 2026. Sitio independiente, sin afiliación con la FIFA." (`es.ts:63-64`). The mock prints "Datos: informes oficiales de rendimiento de partido (PMSR)." — which (a) drops the FIFA non-affiliation clause that OQ-3 makes the *point* of the footer, (b) drops "Copa Mundial 2026", and (c) glosses PMSR (*Post-Match Summary Report*) as "informes de **rendimiento** de partido", i.e. *performance* report. The ruled Spanish deliberately never expands the acronym. Mocks are illustrative, but a wrong gloss in the one artifact an implementer copies from is how it ships. (`mockups/key-landing-mobile.html` `<footer>`.) *Fix:* render `chrome.footer.attribution` unchanged in both locales; if a short form is wanted, use the already-ruled in-panel one — es **"Datos: FIFA PMSR · wc-stats"**, en **"Data: FIFA PMSR · wc-stats"**.
- **[high]** Four supporting lines hardcode corpus counts inside the copy: "Los **104** partidos", "Los **104** del torneo", "Los **1.248** del torneo", "Las **48** selecciones", "**36** tablas de líderes". Two rules collide with this. Voice and Tone: "no hand-formatted numbers" — `Intl.NumberFormat` is mandatory, and "1.248" in `es.ts` beside "1,248" in `en.ts` is exactly a hand-formatted pair. And the shipped precedent is explicit: `leaderboards.teaserCount`'s docblock refuses to hardcode "3" because "a hardcoded three would be a visible lie" (`es.ts:2259-2262`), and solves it with call-site composition on the `search.noResultsBefore`/`…After` idiom, since `t()` has no interpolation. (`mockups/key-landing-mobile.html`, all badge `.sup` lines and the lede.) *Fix:* split every counted line into two fragments and format the number at the call site, e.g. `landing.badge.players.supBefore` es **"Los "** / en **"All "**, `…supAfter` es **" del torneo."** / en **" in the tournament."**; the count comes from the Tournament Index through `Intl.NumberFormat`.
- **[medium]** "Resultados y posiciones, **por fase** y por grupo" reintroduces *fase* for the `Stage` enum, the ambiguity this project spent two stories removing. `viz.table.phase` owns "Fase" for the possession phase, and Juan ruled at the 2026-08-07 review that the `Stage` column unifies on **"Etapa"** on both profile routes precisely because "Fase" was already taken (`es.ts:2571-2573`). The Hub's own `hub.results.empty.headline` ("Sin resultados para esta fase.") is on the losing side of that ruling and has no policy row; the landing badge should not enlarge the split. (`mockups/key-landing-mobile.html` badge 2.) *Fix:* es **"Resultados y posiciones, por etapa y por grupo."**, en **"Results and standings, by stage and by group."** — and add the *stage vs phase* unification to the policy table so the Hub copy gets swept with it.
- **[medium]** "Las 48 **selecciones**" mints a second Spanish name for the term the badge above it calls *Equipos*. The app's ruled noun is **equipo** everywhere it matters — `viz.table.team`, `hub.standings.column.team`, `compare.type.teams` = "Equipos" (`es.ts:2964`), `hub.standings.teamsCount` = "equipos". "Selección" is correct football Spanish for a national side, but introducing it as a synonym on the one surface whose job is to teach the reader what the site's nouns are is the drift the policy table exists to prevent. The `/players` mock repeats it twice ("por selección", "44 selecciones más"). (`mockups/key-landing-mobile.html` badge 6; `mockups/key-players-index-mobile.html` subtitle and overflow line.) *Fix:* badge es **"Los 48 equipos del torneo."**, en **"All 48 teams."**; `/players` subtitle es **"Los 1.248 jugadores del torneo, por equipo."**, en **"All 1,248 players in the tournament, by team."** If *selección* is wanted deliberately, it needs its own policy row saying so.
- **[low]** Lede register wobble: "**Gratis**, independiente y sin publicidad" puts an adverb in a list of adjectives. The shipped `about.project` gets it right — "Es un proyecto personal, **gratuito** y sin publicidad." (`es.ts:108`). Two adjacent surfaces, two forms of one claim. (`mockups/key-landing-mobile.html` `.lede`.) *Fix:* es **"Gratuito, independiente y sin publicidad."**, en **"Free, independent and ad-free."**
- **[low]** "…los 104 partidos del torneo, **leídos de** los informes oficiales" is strained — one reads *a report*, one does not read data *out of* it in this construction. (`mockups/key-landing-mobile.html` `.lede`.) *Fix:* es **"Los 104 partidos del torneo, tomados de los informes oficiales de la FIFA: resultados, posiciones, mapas de tiros, redes de pases y el detalle por jugador."**, en **"All 104 matches of the tournament, taken from FIFA's official reports: results, standings, shot maps, pass networks and the per-player detail."**
- **[low]** The mock's `<h1>` is "Mundial 2026". The spine's zone-1 rule requires "tournament name + site name", and the shipped attribution says "**Copa** Mundial 2026". Two names for the tournament on one page, and the site name is missing. (`mockups/key-landing-mobile.html` `<h1>`; EXPERIENCE.md Landing zone table, zone 1.) *Fix:* es **"Copa Mundial 2026 · WC Stats"**, en **"2026 World Cup · WC Stats"** — `app.siteName` is already locale-invariant and reusable.

## 2. The nine nav labels

- **[high]** Nine nav labels are specified as prose and named as keys nowhere, and **four of the nine have shipped near-twins**: `compare.heading` = "Comparar"/"Compare" (`es.ts:2958`, whose docblock literally says "the visible entry points say 'Comparar'"), `hub.title` = "El torneo"/"The tournament" (`es.ts:625`), `leaderboards.title` = "Líderes del torneo"/"Tournament leaders", `chrome.footer.aboutLink` = "Acerca del sitio"/"About this site". There is no `nav`, `landing`, `players` or `teams` namespace in either dictionary today. Minting nine badge keys plus nine nav keys plus these four twins is eighteen-to-twenty-two homes for nine concepts — precisely the "two sources for one term" the `leaderboards` docblock and Story 2.18's prohibition rule against. (EXPERIENCE.md Navigation, "One destination set, two presentations"; Component Patterns → Navigation menu.) *Fix:* one `nav.destination.*` namespace of nine keys, reused byte-for-byte by both presentations, with the four overlaps pinned in `i18n.test.ts` the way `enums.matchdayRound` is pinned equal to `enums.stage`: `nav.destination.compare` === `compare.heading`, `nav.destination.tops` === a shortened `leaderboards.title` variant ruled here, `nav.destination.about` === `chrome.footer.aboutLink`. Badge and nav then cannot drift by construction, which is what the spine's "the set is identical in both presentations" actually requires.
- **[high]** The nav's own chrome strings are new, user-facing, unkeyed and internally contradictory. The mocks print `aria-label="Menú"` on the **trigger** and `aria-label="Menú"` again on the **sheet region**, a bare `aria-label="Cerrar"` on the close button, and `<nav aria-label="Principal">`. The shipped header-search sheet rules exactly this problem the other way: `search.sheetTitle` = "Buscar en el torneo" exists *because* "a reader who has already activated 'Buscar' learns nothing from a panel called 'Buscar'" (`es.ts:2422-2427`), and the close control is `search.close` = "**Cerrar la búsqueda**", never a bare "Cerrar". The nav reproduces both defects that ruling removed. (`mockups/key-navigation.html` frames A and B; `mockups/key-landing-mobile.html` and `mockups/key-players-index-mobile.html` headers.) *Fix:* trigger es **"Menú"** / en **"Menu"**; sheet title es **"Navegar por el sitio"** / en **"Browse the site"**; close es **"Cerrar el menú"** / en **"Close the menu"**; `<nav>` landmark es **"Navegación principal"** / en **"Main navigation"**.
- **[medium]** The nav mock's search box reads "Buscar jugador, equipo o partido" — a fourth wording for a shipped control. `search.label` = "Busca jugadores, equipos y partidos" and `search.placeholder` = "Escribe un nombre o un partido" (`es.ts:2404-2417`), both authored with recorded reasons, including why `leaderboards.filterPlaceholder` could not be reused. The mock's string is also singular where the ruled label is plural, and imperative-infinitive where the ruled label is tuteo imperative ("Busca"). (`mockups/key-navigation.html` frame A, `.searchbox`.) *Fix:* render `search.placeholder` verbatim — es **"Escribe un nombre o un partido"**, en **"Type a name or a match"**.
- **[low]** `Inicio` / `Home` is correct and needs no change; it is the only one of the nine with no shipped counterpart and no collision. It still needs a policy row (see area 7).
- **[low]** `Comparar` is the only infinitive among eight nouns in the nav. Grammatically a noun set would be tidier ("Comparación"), but "Comparar" is shipped copy with a docblock naming it the entry-point form, and consistency with the shipped surface beats part-of-speech tidiness. *Fix:* none — keep **"Comparar"** / **"Compare"**; log the mixed part of speech in the policy row so it is not re-litigated.

## 3. The L1465 ruled abbreviations (EXPERIENCE.md → "The Expert table at 390 px")

- **[high]** The en `viz.table.shirt` head **"NO." is not an abbreviation of "Shirt"** — it substitutes a different word while `headTitle` and the accessible name still say "Shirt", so the mechanism the ruling invokes does not discharge it. UX-DR17's contract is "the full term behind every ruled abbreviation"; a reader who hovers "NO." is told "Shirt", which explains nothing, because "No." abbreviates *number*, not *shirt*. Compare the working precedent one paragraph away: "VEL. MÁX." → "Velocidad máxima" is the same words, shortened. Two aggravations: en.ts **already ships "No."** as `hub.results.column.matchNumber` with `columnTitle` "Match number" (`en.ts:475`, `en.ts:482`), so this puts one abbreviation on two different numbers site-wide; and all-caps "NO." reads as the English negation. (EXPERIENCE.md L1465 ruling table, row `viz.table.shirt`.) *Fix:* change the **full term**, not just the head, so the abbreviation is honest — es `viz.table.shirt` **"Dorsal"** with head **"Dor."**; en `viz.table.shirt` **"Shirt number"** with head **"No."**. If "Shirt" must remain the en full term, then the en head must remain **"Shirt"** and the ~18 px is not available.
- **[medium]** The abbreviations are ruled in ALL CAPS ("DOR.", "JUG.", "NO."), but the app's table heads render through `type-stat-label`, which carries `text-transform: uppercase` (`app/src/app/globals.css:369-376`; `DataTable.tsx:554,588,613`). `es.ts`'s `leaderboardMetricAbbr` docblock rules this explicitly and gives the worked example: "**SENTENCE CASE, not ALL-CAPS** … the uppercase rendering comes from `type-stat-label` … never from the string", which is why `topSpeed` ships as "Vel. máx." (`es.ts:1985-1991`). Storing "DOR." literally breaks the stated convention and would render identically anyway. (EXPERIENCE.md L1465 ruling table.) *Fix:* store es **"Dor."** and **"Jug."**, en **"No."** and **"Player"** — sentence case; the CSS uppercases.
- **[medium]** es **"JUG." buys no width at all**, so it is a recognisability cost for nothing. A table column is sized by its widest cell, and the player column's cells hold full names — "Ismaël BENNACER" is ~110 px at 15 px, against "JUGADOR" at ~59 px at 11 px caps with 0.08em tracking. The head is never the binding constraint in that column. The en side reached the right answer ("unabbreviated — already short") for a weaker reason than the one that actually applies. This also means the ruling's "abbreviating both identity heads buys perhaps 35 px" should be re-derived: the real saving comes from the **shirt** column alone (~21 px, "DORSAL" ~50 px → "DOR." ~29 px), roughly 40% of what was claimed, which weakens the already-honest "one full column plus a sliver" conclusion further rather than changing it. (EXPERIENCE.md L1465 ruling 1 and ruling 2.) *Fix:* leave `viz.table.player` unabbreviated in **both** locales — es **"Jugador"**, en **"Player"** — and restate the measured saving as shirt-only.
- **[medium]** es "Dor." is defensible but is **not** the conventional Spanish shirt-number abbreviation, and the ruling does not say why it was chosen over the conventional one. Spanish match sheets abbreviate the shirt number as **"N.º"** — which this app already ships, as `hub.results.column.matchNumber` (`es.ts:~745`). Reusing it would put one abbreviation on two different numbers, so "Dor." is the right call; the problem is that the reasoning is not recorded, and "DOR." standing alone is not self-evident to a reader. (EXPERIENCE.md L1465 ruling table.) *Fix:* keep es **"Dor."** (full term "Dorsal") and add the policy row recording the rejection of "N.º" on collision grounds — the same discipline the *stage column* row already applies to "Etapa"/"Fase".
- **[low]** The ruling table's en-head cell reads "**PLAYER** (unabbreviated — already short)". "PLAYER" is the rendered form, not the string; writing it that way in the spine invites someone to store it uppercase and break the casing rule above. *Fix:* write the cell as **"Player"** and note that `type-stat-label` uppercases it.

## 4. The `/players` position abbreviations (POR / DEF / MED / DEL)

- **[critical]** **"POR" abbreviates *portero* — the word this spine explicitly rejected.** The policy table's *goalkeeper* row rules "arquero … 'portero' reads peninsular/Mexican for this audience", the *positions* row rules the set as "arquero / defensa / mediocampista / delantero", and `enums.position.gk` ships **"Arquero"** (`es.ts:1549`). So the ruled abbreviation is an abbreviation of the rejected term, and UX-DR17's "the full term behind every abbreviation" is unsatisfiable: expanding "POR" to "Arquero" is not an expansion. DEF / MED / DEL are correct against their full terms; only the goalkeeper is wrong, and it is the one position this project already ruled twice. (EXPERIENCE.md "The Player and Team Indexes" → Position bullet; `mockups/key-players-index-mobile.html` row cells.) *Fix:* es **ARQ / DEF / MED / DEL**, expanding to Arquero / Defensa / Mediocampista / Delantero.
- **[high]** **The en counterparts are specified nowhere** — not in the spine's `/players` bullet, not in the policy table, not in the Responsive row. English needs an abbreviation set too, since the column is equally narrow at 390 px in en. (EXPERIENCE.md "The Player and Team Indexes"; no row in the policy table.) *Fix:* en **GK / DF / MF / FW** — byte-identical to the contract's own `position` enum codes (`"gk" | "df" | "mf" | "fw"`) and the FIFA team-list set, so nothing is invented and no second English position vocabulary enters the app. Do **not** use GK/DEF/MID/FWD, which would be a third form beside the enum codes and `enums.position.*`.
- **[high]** **A header tooltip cannot discharge a cell-level abbreviation.** The spine says position "renders as a ruled abbreviation … with the full term in the **header's tooltip and accessible name**". But the abbreviation is in the *cells*: hovering the head "POS." yields "Posición", which never tells the reader that "MED" is "Mediocampista". Every ruled abbreviation in the app so far has been a *column head*, where head-tooltip is the correct mechanism; this is the first cell-level one and it inherits a mechanism that does not fit. (EXPERIENCE.md "The Player and Team Indexes" → Position bullet.) *Fix:* rule the cell mechanism explicitly — each cell renders `<abbr title={t('enums.position.' + code)}>` with an `sr-only` full term so the accessible name is the full word, and the **head** keeps the full term (see the next finding). Both locales, same shape.
- **[high]** This is the **first abbreviated rendering of `enums.position.*` anywhere in the app**, and the delta neither says so nor names a key. Every current call site renders the full label, including a dense 390 px table: `ExpertLayer.tsx:519`, `LineupsDisclosure.tsx:49`, `PlayerHero.tsx:45`, `app/players/[slug]/page.tsx:77`. A second display form for one shipped enum needs its own namespace and its own pin, exactly as `leaderboardMetricAbbr` does for the two metric abbreviations (`es.ts:1990`, with `i18n.test.ts` pinning them to their full-term twins so "a second mint cannot drift in beside them"). *Fix:* mint `enums.positionAbbr.{gk,df,mf,fw}` in both dictionaries — es **ARQ / DEF / MED / DEL**, en **GK / DF / MF / FW** — pin each against its `enums.position.*` twin in `i18n.test.ts`, and state in the spine that `enums.position.*` stays unabbreviated at every other call site.
- **[medium]** The mock's column head "**Pos.**" is byte-identical to `hub.standings.column.rank` (`es.ts:703`), whose `columnTitle` is "**Posición**" meaning *league position*. One abbreviation, two meanings, two routes — the collision the *stage column* row mints "Etapa" to avoid. And nothing forces it: `viz.table.position` = "Posición"/"Position" already ships (`es.ts:1045`, `en.ts:615`), and at 11 px caps "POSICIÓN" is ~76 px inside a 358 px content width whose only other column is the name. (`mockups/key-players-index-mobile.html` `<th scope="col">Pos.</th>`.) *Fix:* head reuses `viz.table.position` unabbreviated — es **"Posición"**, en **"Position"**; only the cells abbreviate.
- **[medium]** Unowned i18n consequence of the pass-through rule on a *browse* surface: the 48 group headings are raw artifact names in English ("Algeria", "Argentina", "Belgium", "Brazil" in the mock) in artifact order, so a Spanish reader browsing for *Alemania* must know to look under **G** for "Germany", and the name filter is player-name-scoped. Data pass-through is correct and not in question (FR-30) — but `/players` is the first surface whose *primary organising axis* is untranslated data, and this spine owns consequences elsewhere (single-tree i18n, `/teams` redundancy) rather than discovering them. (EXPERIENCE.md "`/players` — grouped by team"; `mockups/key-players-index-mobile.html`.) *Fix:* no string change — record the consequence in the spine, and rule that the name filter also matches the team-group heading, so typing "Germany" and typing a player name both work. If a Spanish reader must be served on the team axis, that is a data-layer decision (a `nameEs` field), not an i18n one, and belongs in the ledger.

## 5. Spanish text expansion — the `lg` inline-nav assumption

- **[high]** **The estimate fails on its own arithmetic.** The shipped header row is `mx-auto flex min-h-14 max-w-6xl … px-gutter-desktop` (`app/src/components/SiteHeader.tsx:132`) with `--spacing-gutter-desktop: 1.5rem` (`globals.css:303`). At exactly 1024 px, `max-w-6xl` (1152 px) does not bind, so the usable measure is **1024 − 48 = 976 px**. The delta's own table totals **~1,010 px**. It is short by ~34 px before anyone re-measures anything. (EXPERIENCE.md Navigation → "`≥lg`, not `≥md`"; `mockups/key-navigation.html` frame C width table.) *Fix:* no string change; see the threshold recommendation below.
- **[high]** **The nine-label figure is low.** The table allots "9 enlaces ES + 8 espacios = ~552", i.e. ~440 px of text across *Inicio, Comparar, Torneo, Partidos, Tops, Jugadores, Equipos, Glosario, Acerca de* (61 characters plus one space). At Inter 400 / 15 px — `type-body`, which frame C's own CSS uses for the inline links — those nine labels measure **~490–510 px**, so the row runs **~1,060–1,080 px**, ~85–105 px over the 976 px available. Every correct fix in this report makes it worse, not better: "Líderes" over "Tops" adds ~20 px, "Acerca del sitio" over "Acerca de" adds ~45 px. Corrected labels put the row near **~1,145 px**. (`mockups/key-navigation.html` frame C.) *Fix:* no string change; the labels are not the thing to shorten — shortening them is what produced findings 1 and 2.
- **[high]** **Frame C models a control the app does not ship**, and the arithmetic depends on the substitution. The mock renders the header search as `.searchinline{width:190px; flex:none}`. The shipped slot is `min-w-0 flex-1` (`HeaderSearch.tsx:373`, kept deliberately from Story 2.2 and annotated as what "lets the input shrink inside the header's flex row"). With `flex-1` the row does not overflow at all — the search input **collapses toward zero width**, silently, and the width table cannot show that failure mode because it assumes the opposite behaviour. This matters because the reflow guard R2/D8 tests for document overflow, which this failure would not trigger. *Fix:* no string change — either give the search slot a `min-w` floor so the overflow becomes visible and the ruled fallback fires, or state in the spine that the `≥lg` slot changes from `flex-1` to a fixed measure. Do not leave it implied by a mock.
- **[medium]** The delta's ruled fallback (*Glosario* and *Acerca de* drop to an overflow trigger) recovers roughly **150 px** with current labels and ~195 px with corrected ones, which brings the corrected row to ~950 px and fits 976 px — but only just, and only in Spanish at exactly 1024 px with no user font scaling. *Fix:* adopt one of two, and rule it rather than leaving it conditional: (a) make the fallback the **default** — seven inline destinations at `lg`, all nine at `xl`; or (b) set the inline threshold to **`xl` (1280 px)**, where the usable measure is 1280 − 48 = **1,232 px** and absorbs the corrected ~1,145 px with real margin. Option (b) is the honest one: it keeps "one destination set, two presentations" intact instead of introducing a third, partial presentation.
- **[low]** "Inglés no es el caso difícil … si entra en español, entra en inglés" is right in direction and worth keeping, but the ~20–30 % figure is a document-level average; for these nine short labels the real es/en delta is closer to ~18 % (Comparar/Compare, Torneo/Tournament — the latter is *longer* in English). The conclusion holds; the number should not be reused as a measurement. *Fix:* none — reword to "measured at the shipped scale, en is shorter overall for this label set".

## 6. Register and tone of the new copy

- **[pass]** The disclosure control pair is exactly right. "Ver los jugadores" / "Ocultar los jugadores" is the shipped canonical shape — `viz.viewData`/`hideData` = "Ver los datos"/"Ocultar los datos" (`es.ts:949-950`) and `leaderboards.showTable`/`hideTable` = "Ver la tabla"/"Ocultar la tabla" (`es.ts:2300-2301`): verb + definite article + noun, matched Ver/Ocultar pair. No ad-hoc variant, no "Click aquí". Tuteo holds throughout, there are no exclamation marks anywhere in the delta, and no mixed-language strings.
- **[medium]** The en counterparts of the disclosure pair are unspecified, and the shipped en dictionary does **not** map "Ver" to one English verb: `viz.viewData` is "View data" while `leaderboards.showTable` is "**Show** the table" (`en.ts:562`, `en.ts:1242`). `/players` is a disclosure over rows, so it follows the second, not the first. (`mockups/key-players-index-mobile.html` `.disc` buttons; no en mock exists.) *Fix:* en **"Show the players"** / **"Hide the players"**.
- **[medium]** The filter reuses the shipped `leaderboards.filter*` **keys** on a non-leaderboard route. The wording is genuinely true here (unlike the header search, where Story 2.14 declined the same reuse because the copy was *false* about a control that also finds matches), so the copy is right — but `leaderboards.filterLabel` carries a namespace that scopes it to boards, and `/players` is not a board. Precedent from `leaderboards.scope.*` is that namespace scoping is treated as load-bearing, not cosmetic.
