# Accessibility Review — 2026-08-26 delta

Adversarial WCAG 2.1 AA review of the 2026-08-26 additions to `EXPERIENCE.md` + `DESIGN.md`: the Landing page, the navigation menu, `/players` and `/teams`, the Deep-Link Fragment Grammar, and the re-ruled site header — plus the three new mocks (`key-landing-mobile.html`, `key-navigation.html`, `key-players-index-mobile.html`). Scope is the delta only. Nothing closed by `review-accessibility.md` (2026-07-21) is re-reported; that review's two criticals — the heatmap's lowest step and the light-theme focus ring on the pitch — are verified closed in the frontmatter (`heat-1` now computes 3.68:1, `focus-ring-on-pitch` #EAFBFD computes 11.45:1, the heat ramp is now strictly monotonic in luminance, `result-chip-ink` exists). All contrast ratios below are computed from the declared hex tokens by the WCAG relative-luminance formula, not taken from the documents' assertions.

## Overall verdict

**The shipped AA floor does not hold as written.** Most of the delta extends it faithfully — the explicit "not a `role="menu"`" ruling and its reasoning, Esc-returns-focus-to-trigger, `aria-current` never by colour alone, `prefers-reduced-motion`, the 44 px floor, DOM-order-equals-visual-order, and a heading hierarchy on `/players` that is correct — but two defects are mechanical rather than arguable: the navigation sheet's **modality is self-contradictory inside a single section** and contradicts the shipped Story 2.14 sheet it claims to follow (which is a modal Radix Dialog that inerts the document), and the trigger's **unconditional `aria-controls`** is a dangling IDREF on a control that appears on all 1,410 routes — an `aria-valid-attr-value` failure that would turn the axe-clean gate red in all 32 of its runs.

Below those: `/players` ships 48 unnamed tables and 48 identically-named disclosure triggers with an unannounced filter count; the fragment grammar moves focus to the *outer* layer for a two-layer fragment, which is the failure the contract opens by naming; and the two new lime tokens (`nav-menu.current-marker-color`, `feature-badge.emphasis-border`) carry no light-theme value, computing **1.12–1.28:1** if the unsuffixed dark token ships literally. Every finding is a sentence- or token-level fix; the architecture of the delta is sound and its reasoning is unusually well recorded.

## 1. The navigation menu

Verified good: trigger is a real `<button>` with `aria-expanded`; `Enter`/`Space` opens; `Esc` closes the topmost layer and returns focus to the trigger; no trap when closed; reading order equals DOM order equals visual order; the "not a `role="menu"`" ruling is correct *and* carries its reason (arrow-key-only navigation would break the UX-DR15 reading-order tab model); `aria-current="page"` in both presentations with a non-colour cue in each (lime marker **plus** weight change in the sheet, underline **plus** weight inline); `prefers-reduced-motion` disables the transition entirely with the honest note that motion here is decorative; every accessible name declared a locale key in both dictionaries; the nav explicitly added as a path rather than substituted for any existing one.

- **[critical]** **Focus containment is self-contradictory, and both readings are wrong as specified.** The sheet-geometry bullet argues that a *full-screen* sheet "needs a focus trap over the whole viewport ... which is neither what was built"; the Keyboard-model bullet two lines later says "`Tab` **cycles within the sheet** while open" — which is a focus trap. The mock renders the non-modal reading (`<div class="sheet" role="region" aria-label="Menú">`, page behind explicitly still visible and not inerted) while carrying the trapping keyboard model. Meanwhile the shipped sheet this one says it "follows" is a **modal Radix Dialog**: `app/src/components/HeaderSearch.tsx:390` states "the dialog is MODAL, so Radix marks the rest of the document inert", stamping `aria-hidden="true"` on every body sibling — so what was built *is* a trapping, document-inerting modal whose geometry merely is not full-screen. As written an implementer builds a hand-rolled focus trap inside a non-modal, non-inerted `role="region"`: keyboard users are confined while pointer and virtual-cursor users are not, and no `role="dialog"`/`aria-modal`/`inert` exposes the confinement (EXPERIENCE.md → Navigation, sheet-geometry + keyboard-model bullets; mockups/key-navigation.html Frame A; WCAG 2.1.2, 4.1.2, 2.4.3). *Fix:* separate geometry from modality in one sentence — "the nav sheet is the shipped modal sheet: `role="dialog"` + `aria-modal`, a `DialogTitle` from a locale key, the rest of the document inert, `Tab` cycles inside, `Esc` returns focus to the trigger. Full-**width** describes its geometry, not its modality." Delete the parenthetical that implies the shipped sheet does not trap.
- **[critical]** **`aria-controls` as ruled is a dangling IDREF on every route.** The keyboard model says flatly "trigger is a real `<button>` with `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls`", and all three mocks render `aria-controls="navsheet"` with the sheet closed (in two of them the sheet is not in the DOM at all). A portalled sheet does not exist while closed, so the IDREF resolves to nothing — axe `aria-valid-attr-value`, on the site header, i.e. on 1,410 routes × 2 themes × 2 locales. The codebase already knows this: `HeaderSearch.tsx:1000` ships `aria-controls={listboxRendered ? listboxId : undefined}` with a comment naming exactly this failure (EXPERIENCE.md → Navigation, keyboard model; all three mocks; WCAG 4.1.2, and the axe-clean acceptance floor). *Fix:* rule the conditional form — `aria-controls` present only while the sheet is rendered — or rule that the sheet's container is always mounted.
- **[high]** **Search inside the sheet is a depth-2 overlay stack, which UX-DR15 bans and the test suite pins.** Below `lg` the nav sheet absorbs search, but Component Patterns still says of Header search "On `<md`: full-width sheet, same semantics" — so activating search inside the nav sheet opens a second sheet inside the first. UX-DR15 bans "modal stacks >1 deep"; `HeaderSearch.tsx:756` implements a page-wide single overlay slot ("closing whatever else held it") and `HeaderSearch.test.tsx:643` pins it in both directions. Nothing in the delta resolves the collision (EXPERIENCE.md → Navigation; Component Patterns → Header search; WCAG 2.4.3, plus the project's own overlay-depth invariant). *Fix:* rule that inside the nav sheet, search renders **inline** — its combobox and listbox in-panel, taking no second overlay slot — and that the `<md` search sheet exists only where the nav sheet is not the container.
- **[high]** **Moving the language toggle into the sheet silences its live region.** The `<lg` composition moves language and theme into the sheet, but the language toggle's ruled polite announcement ("Idioma: Español" / "Language: English", 4.1.3) is a header node — and a modal sheet `aria-hidden`s the header. This is not hypothetical: `HeaderSearch.tsx:375-395` records the identical defect being found and fixed at code review, the sheet now carrying its own live region *inside the portal* because "the two can never both speak." The delta moves a live-region-bearing control into that same portal and says nothing (EXPERIENCE.md → Navigation; Component Patterns → Language toggle; WCAG 4.1.3). *Fix:* state that the sheet carries its own polite live region inside the portal for any announcement raised by a control it contains — and rule the three unanswered questions that follow: does the sheet stay open across a locale change, do its own nine link names swap under the reader, and where does focus sit afterwards.
- **[high]** **Whether the two presentations are one DOM tree or two is never ruled**, and every consequence is an accessibility one. Two trees means two `<nav>` landmarks with the same accessible name, two sets of nine links with identical names, two elements carrying `aria-current="page"`, and a duplicated sheet id — the last being precisely the defect this contract calls out elsewhere ("two elements carrying that id would be a duplicate-id defect"). One tree means the trigger must be `display:none` at `≥lg` rather than visually hidden (EXPERIENCE.md → Navigation, "two presentations"; Responsive & Platform → Site header composition; WCAG 1.3.1, 4.1.2). *Fix:* one sentence — one tree, one `<nav>`, the trigger and the sheet removed from the accessibility tree at `≥lg`, the inline list removed below it.
- **[high]** **Two nav entries resolve to the same page and `aria-current` has no collision rule.** *Torneo* → `/tournament` and *Partidos* → `/tournament#results` are both "current" when the reader is on `/tournament`; the spec says only "current route is marked with `aria-current="page"` in both presentations". As written, either two entries carry `aria-current="page"` (invalid — there is one current page) or the fragment entry never marks and the reader arriving from *Partidos* sees nothing marked. The landing grid has the same containment (badge 3 is contained by badge 2, recorded and accepted) but badges carry no `aria-current`, so the defect is nav-only (EXPERIENCE.md → Navigation; The Landing Page → ruled badge set; WCAG 4.1.2). *Fix:* `aria-current="page"` on the page-level entry only; the fragment entry takes none, or `aria-current="location"` while its section is the one in view.
- **[medium]** **"Focus moves to the sheet's first focusable element" rules by position, and the position is a text input.** As composed (mock Frame A) the first focusable in the sheet is the search box, so every menu opening lands a reader — and a mobile keyboard — in a search field on a control they opened to navigate (EXPERIENCE.md → Navigation, keyboard model; mockups/key-navigation.html Frame A; WCAG 2.4.3). *Fix:* name the target rather than deriving it from order — the sheet container (`tabindex="-1"`, labelled by its title) or the first nav link, whichever is ruled, so recomposing the sheet cannot silently move focus.
- **[medium]** **A second dismiss control exists in the mock and in no rule.** The sheet carries its own "Cerrar" button; the spec rules focus return for `Esc` only. Nothing says where focus goes when the close button is used, when the trigger is pressed a second time, on outside click, or on focus-out — and for a sheet that (per the geometry bullet) leaves the page behind visible and clickable, outside-click dismissal is the likeliest interaction of all (mockups/key-navigation.html Frame A; EXPERIENCE.md → Navigation; WCAG 2.4.3). *Fix:* rule all four paths as returning focus to the trigger, and add the close control to the enumerated set of accessible names.
- **[medium]** **Content-driven height with no max-height or overflow rule fails the delta's own 320 px reflow floor.** Nine 44 px links + a 44 px search row + the language/theme footer ≈ 500 CSS px. The Accessibility Floor commits reflow to 320 CSS px, whose 1.4.10 reference viewport is 320 × 256; a 390 px phone at 200 % zoom is 195 × ~422. In both, the sheet's footer — holding the language and theme controls the `<lg` composition just moved there — is below the fold, and a `top: 0` sheet is normally fixed-positioned, in which case it is not merely below the fold but unreachable. The shipped sheet answers this with `max-h-dvh` + `overflow-y-auto` (`HeaderSearch.tsx:737`); the nav spec does not carry it forward, and mock Frame A is a fixed-width box with no height constraint, so it demonstrates nothing about this (EXPERIENCE.md → Navigation, sheet geometry; Accessibility Floor; WCAG 1.4.10, 2.1.1). *Fix:* state `max-h-dvh` + internal scroll explicitly, and make the acceptance a 320 × 256 render with the sheet open in Spanish.
- **[medium]** **The `≥lg` row breaks the delta's own 44 px floor.** The keyboard model says "every target ≥44×44 px (`MIN_HIT_PX`)", and Component Patterns says the header "keeps 44 px through the wrap" — but mock Frame C renders inline links 44 px tall and only as wide as their label (~34 px for *Tops*) with 14 px gaps, an inline search box at 40 px, and ES|EN segments at 40 px min-height (mockups/key-navigation.html Frame C; EXPERIENCE.md → Navigation; project `MIN_HIT_PX` floor, WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8). *Fix:* say whether the 44 px floor binds the `≥lg` row; if it does, the mock's widths are the thing to change, and the ~1,010 px arithmetic must be recomputed with padded targets.
- **[medium]** **The ruled `≥lg` fallback is an unspecified second overlay.** "*Glosario* and *Acerca de* drop to an overflow trigger first" is ruled as the escape hatch if the `lg` assumption fails, but that trigger has no keyboard model, no aria, no focus rule, and no relationship to the single-overlay-slot registry — and it would sit in a header row that already owns one overlay. Because it is the *ruled* fallback, it ships unspecified the moment the measurement goes the wrong way (EXPERIENCE.md → Navigation, `≥lg` bullet; WCAG 4.1.2). *Fix:* one sentence giving the overflow trigger the same model as the `<lg` trigger, or make the fallback the default (see §2).
- **[low]** **Trigger and sheet share one accessible name** ("Menú" in the mock). The shipped search sheet ruled the opposite case explicitly — "a reader who has just activated *Buscar* learns nothing from a panel called *Buscar*" — and minted a distinct title. *Fix:* give the sheet its own title key and note the precedent.
- **[low]** `prefers-reduced-motion` is ruled for the sheet transition only. A nav entry that deep-links into a match section triggers the fragment grammar's scroll, whose reduced-motion behaviour lives in another section. *Fix:* cross-reference rather than restate half of it.

## 2. The header at wrapped widths

- **[high]** **The 2.4.7 consequence of the accepted defect is not merely under-stated — it is absent, and the delta creates new paths into it.** `app/src/app/globals.css:446` records the measurement without euphemism: the header is 62 px one-row, **118 px where the row wraps** (below ~342 px es / ~338 px en) and **124 px at a 195 px layout viewport**, against `scroll-padding-top: 4.5rem` (72 px) — so "a wrapped bar **OVERLAPS an anchored heading by 46 px** — it is hidden, not merely tight." The delta's own Deep-Link Fragment Grammar then rules that **focus moves to that heading** on every resolution, and rules that nav entries pointing into match-route sections use the same path. A programmatically focused heading whose focus indicator is entirely behind an opaque sticky bar is a **2.4.7 Focus Visible** failure (2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured in WCAG 2.2), at 320 px Spanish — a width the Accessibility Floor explicitly commits to. `EXPERIENCE.md` names the shortfall exactly once, parenthetically, inside the width arithmetic, and only to argue that a *fifth* header element would widen the affected band (EXPERIENCE.md → Navigation, width arithmetic; Deep-Link Fragment Grammar, keyboard parity bullet; WCAG 2.4.7). *Fix:* state the defect, its 2.4.7 exposure and its width band in both sections; the `--header-h` custom property that `globals.css` already names as "the honest fix" is a shared-contract change, and this delta — which adds the nav *and* ports the fragment grammar — is the change-set with cause to make it.
- **[medium]** **The improvement is claimed but its only load-bearing number is missing.** The delta asserts the `<lg` row's min-content now falls below the 237 px R2/D8 figure and that "the 320 px row gets easier, not harder" — but never states the consequence that decides whether the defect survives: **the new wrap threshold**. If it falls below 320 px, the 118 px case disappears on phones and the accepted defect is *closed* for the committed floor; if it does not, it persists. Mock Frame B asserts the composition "reduces but does not close" it, and Frame B is a fixed-width 195 px box that can demonstrate neither (EXPERIENCE.md → Navigation; Responsive & Platform → Site header composition; mockups/key-navigation.html Frame B). *Fix:* state the predicted threshold, and make the re-run R2/D8 matrix (320/390/195 × 2 themes × 2 locales) its acceptance rather than a note.
- **[medium]** **The `≥lg` arithmetic has no margin, and overflowing it re-creates the defect at widths that do not have it today.** The mock totals the row at **~1,010 px**; at exactly `lg` (1024 px) the usable measure after `gutter-desktop` on both sides is **976 px**. The mock calls the margin "deliberately narrow"; computed, it is negative. If the row wraps at 1024–1152 px, the desktop header becomes a two-row sticky bar — 118 px against 72 px — and the anchored-heading overlap appears on desktop, where it has never existed (mockups/key-navigation.html Frame C; EXPERIENCE.md → Navigation, `≥lg` bullet; WCAG 2.4.7 as above). *Fix:* raise the inline threshold to `xl`, or invert the ruling so the seven-link row plus overflow is the default and inline-nine is the enhancement above a measured width.

## 3. The feature badges

Verified good: real `<a href>` (middle-click, open-in-new-tab, browser link list all work — stated explicitly and worth the sentence); the whole card is one tab stop; the emphasis is redundantly encoded by size **and** position **and** border, so it survives grayscale; no fill-or-hue emphasis anywhere, and the Do/Don't table pins it.

- **[medium]** **A screen-reader user does lose the emphasis, and the spec resolves the tension by assertion rather than by design.** `DESIGN.md` says the emphasis survives "a screen reader that sees none of it"; `EXPERIENCE.md` says the badge "carries no 'featured'-type meaning that a screen reader would miss." But the same document calls it "**The one destination that gets its own row and visual weight**." Both cannot hold. 1.4.1 is satisfied — nothing is colour-only — but if the emphasis carries meaning, 1.3.1 wants that meaning programmatically determinable, and the only channel that survives is DOM position (first), which carries it weakly and only to a reader who happens to notice ordinal position (EXPERIENCE.md → The Landing Page, zone 2; DESIGN.md → Components → Feature badge; WCAG 1.3.1). *Fix:* pick one. Either give zone 2 its own heading (see §6) so the distinction has a programmatic name, or restate the emphasis as visual-only and drop the "no meaning a screen reader would miss" claim, which is doing argumentative work the design does not support.
- **[medium]** **The ruled accessible name is internally impossible.** Component Patterns: "one accessible name, that name being **the visible label** (2.5.3)". The Landing Page: "accessible names are the visible label; **no `aria-label` narrows or extends them**." As mocked, the link's name-from-content is label *plus* supporting line — "Comparar Dos jugadores, dos equipos o dos partidos, lado a lado." Either the supporting line is in the name (so the name is not "the visible label") or an `aria-label` narrows it (forbidden by the second sentence, and it would drop the supporting line out of the accessibility tree entirely). 2.5.3 passes either way, since the name contains the visible label — but the rule as written cannot be implemented (EXPERIENCE.md → Component Patterns → Feature badge, and The Landing Page; mockups/key-landing-mobile.html; WCAG 2.5.3, 4.1.2). *Fix:* rule the name as the full card content (label then supporting line), and delete "that name being the visible label" in favour of "the visible label is the *first* thing in the name, and no `aria-label` replaces it."
- **[medium]** **The card boundary is effectively invisible, and it is the only thing conveying the target's extent.** Computed: hairline border vs the card **1.31:1** dark / **1.23:1** light; hairline vs the page **1.44:1** dark; the card-vs-page tonal step itself **1.09:1** dark / **1.07:1** light. `DESIGN.md` bans icons, illustration and gradient on badges and gives the label no link styling, so nothing but that boundary says "this whole rectangle is one link" — and the same document already accepted this argument once, adding a border to dark-theme pitch panels because a 1.55:1 edge was "too soft for low-vision users" (DESIGN.md → Components → Feature badge; Elevation & Depth; WCAG 1.4.11, 1.4.1 for link identification). *Fix:* raise the badge border to ≥3:1 against the page in both themes, or state that the label is the identifiable affordance and give it link styling.
- **[low]** The grid is loose anchors in a `<div>` — no list semantics, no group name, no count (see §6).

## 4. The deep-link fragment grammar

Verified good: the `section` / `section-panel` shape with a stable authored suffix, and the explicit reasoning for why a `useId()` value cannot serve (it is absent from the DOM while the region is closed); every disclosure addressable so `#shot-maps`' two-disclosure collision is closed; re-entry ruled behavioural; one mechanism rather than two; `aria-expanded` reflecting the opened state; `prefers-reduced-motion` honoured.

- **[high]** **For a two-layer fragment, focus lands on the outer layer — the exact failure the contract opens by naming.** The keyboard-parity bullet says "focus moves to the revealed **section** heading", singular. `#shot-maps-shot-log` opens two layers: the section shell and the disclosure inside it. Focus therefore lands on the section heading with the opened panel somewhere below it, possibly off-screen — which is verbatim the defect the contract exists to close ("a shared link that lands a reader on a heading above a closed control has not delivered them to the data"). The `section`-only case is fine; the `section-panel` case, which is the one the grammar was minted for, is not (EXPERIENCE.md → Deep-Link Fragment Grammar; WCAG 2.4.3). *Fix:* focus the **deepest resolved layer** — for `section-panel`, the panel's disclosure trigger; for `section`, the section heading.
- **[medium]** **"The focus move is the announcement" is sufficient only if the focused element carries the state, and a heading does not.** `aria-expanded` lives on the trigger the reader has just been moved *past*, so what a screen reader announces is a heading name and nothing about anything having opened. 4.1.3 does not bite (focus moved, so this is not a status message), but the state exposed under 4.1.2 never reaches the user (EXPERIENCE.md → Deep-Link Fragment Grammar; WCAG 4.1.2). *Fix:* focusing the disclosure trigger (per the finding above) resolves both at once — its name plus "expanded" is announced, and the content sits immediately below it.
- **[medium]** **The production fallback is silent by design.** An unresolvable `section-panel` "falls back to the nearest resolvable ancestor — the section". A reader who followed a link to the cross log lands on the shot-maps section with the cross log still closed and nothing said, which is a quieter version of the `#shot-maps-log` failure the contract cites as its motivating case. "Development must say so" is a developer affordance, not a user one (EXPERIENCE.md → Deep-Link Fragment Grammar; WCAG 4.1.3). *Fix:* on fallback, focus the section heading **and** announce politely that the specific panel could not be opened — one locale key, both dictionaries.
- **[medium]** The focused heading lands under the sticky bar at wrapped widths — see §2, and note that this grammar is the mechanism that makes the accepted defect user-visible on a keyboard path rather than only on a scroll path.
- **[low]** Reduced motion is ruled for the transition, but the scroll mechanism on resolution (`scroll-padding-top` vs an explicit `scrollIntoView` with `block`) is not named here even though it decides where the focused heading ends up. *Fix:* name it, since §2's fix has to touch the same value.

## 5. `/players` (and `/teams`)

Verified good: `h1` → `h2`-per-team hierarchy is correct and consistent; counts rendered **outside** each disclosure, so the shape of the tournament is readable before anything opens; `Intl.Collator('es', {sensitivity:'base'})` for accent- and case-insensitive matching; two columns at 390 px with no name truncated; every row links to its profile; the filter reuses the shipped idiom rather than minting a second one; the rejected groupings are recorded with reasons.

- **[high]** **The filter's result count has no live region.** "It filters the whole set ... and **reports its result count**" — a count that changes without a focus change is the definition of a status message. This spine specifies a polite live region for sort-direction changes, for the language toggle, and for bundle load; not for this one (EXPERIENCE.md → The Player and Team Indexes; WCAG 4.1.3). *Fix:* `role="status"` on the count, debounced, and rule what it counts (matching players, or matching players across N teams).
- **[high]** **Forty-eight tables, none of them named.** The mock gives each table `Pos. / Jugador` column heads and no `<caption>`, no `aria-label`, no `aria-labelledby`; the team name is an `<h2>` *outside* the table, and the `<section>` wrapping the pair has no `aria-labelledby`, so it is not a named region either. In a screen reader's table list, all 48 read identically and none says which squad it is (mockups/key-players-index-mobile.html; EXPERIENCE.md → The Player and Team Indexes; WCAG 1.3.1, 2.4.6). *Fix:* a `<caption>` per table (or `aria-labelledby` to the team `<h2>`) composed from a locale key plus the team name, and `aria-labelledby` on each `<section>` so the 48 groups are named regions.
- **[high]** **Forty-eight disclosure triggers with the identical accessible name.** "Ver los jugadores", repeated 48 times, with no programmatic association to the `<h2>` above it. A reader listing buttons gets 48 indistinguishable controls (mockups/key-players-index-mobile.html; WCAG 4.1.2, 2.4.6). *Fix:* compose the team name into the accessible name, or `aria-labelledby="{teamHeadingId} {buttonId}"`.
- **[high]** **The abbreviation rule expands the wrong thing, and two of the four abbreviations are Spanish function words.** The spec puts "the full term in the **header's** tooltip and accessible name" — so `Pos.` expands to *Posición*, and nothing anywhere expands `POR` to *arquero*. An es-MX/es-ES TTS reads `POR` as the preposition *por* and `DEL` as the contraction *del*: the cells are not merely opaque, they are actively misread as connective words in a two-column list of names. The rule being cited is also explicitly scoped — "Abbreviate **only** in the Expert per-player tables at `<md`" — so `/players` invoking it is outside its own bounds, and the **EN abbreviations do not exist anywhere in either spine** (EXPERIENCE.md → The Player and Team Indexes; Responsive & Platform → ruled abbreviations; WCAG 1.3.1; 3.1.4 is AAA, but the homograph makes this a comprehension defect at AA altitude). *Fix:* rule the position cell's accessible name as the full ruled term from `enums.position.*` (*arquero / defensa / mediocampista / delantero*) with the abbreviation display-only, widen the abbreviation rule's scope explicitly, and mint the EN pair.
- **[medium]** **What filtering does to the 48 groups is unruled, and every option has a screen-reader consequence.** The filter "filters the whole set, not only what is open" — but nothing says whether non-matching teams are removed (the heading outline silently drops from 48 `h2`s to N), shown with a zero count, or left untouched, nor whether matching teams auto-expand. As written a reader can type a name, hear "3 jugadores" (once §5's live region exists), and find nothing visible has changed (EXPERIENCE.md → The Player and Team Indexes; WCAG 1.3.1, 4.1.3, and the contract's own SM-C2 "nothing is deleted"). *Fix:* rule it — matching groups auto-expand, non-matching groups collapse and remain listed with a zero count — and say the outline is stable.
- **[medium]** **Row link targets are the name text only**, ~20 px tall as mocked, against the spine's own `MIN_HIT_PX` 44 px floor and the shipped `RowAnchor` component that exists to enforce it (`app/src/components/RowAnchor.tsx:48`) (mockups/key-players-index-mobile.html; project 44 px floor, WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8). *Fix:* state that index rows use `RowAnchor`.
- **[medium]** **`/teams` is one sentence of spec and no mock.** "Name, group, and record (played–won–drawn–lost)" rules no markup (table or list), no column heads, no accessible name, and leaves the record as a compound cell that a screen reader reads as four bare numbers — the same problem the standings table solved elsewhere with labelled columns. It is a new route on the axe-clean matrix with nothing ruled for it (EXPERIENCE.md → The Player and Team Indexes; WCAG 1.3.1). *Fix:* rule it as a `<table>` with a caption and four labelled columns, or give the compound cell a composed accessible name.
- **[low]** The mock's filter is `<label for="pf">` pointing at a `<div>`; the spec should say the control is a real `<input type="search">` with a persistent **visible** label rather than a placeholder — the mock's "Escribe un nombre" sits inside the box and would disappear on input.
- **[low]** The disclosure's name changes with state ("Ver" ↔ "Ocultar los jugadores") while `aria-expanded` also changes — the exact opposite of the rule this same delta writes for the nav trigger ("the trigger's name is **stable** across open and closed states; `aria-expanded` carries the state"). Two idioms for one pattern inside one contract; worth reconciling or explicitly exempting the shipped one.

## 6. The landing page

Verified good: **DOM order equals visual order at every ruled width** — the claim holds as mocked, since the emphasised badge is a full-width block above a row-flow grid that reflows 1 → 2 → 4 columns without reordering; real `<a href>` badges; `<main>` and `<footer>` landmarks present; nothing behind a disclosure and no table, so a first arrival's screen reader reaches the complete surface without opening anything; no horizontal scroll at 390 px in Spanish, the longer locale.

- **[medium]** **One heading on the whole page.** The mock has `<h1>Mundial 2026</h1>` and nothing else — zones 2 and 3, the eight controls that are the entire purpose of the surface, carry no heading and no group name. A reader navigating by heading gets one heading and stops, on the route that exists to answer "where do I go" (mockups/key-landing-mobile.html; EXPERIENCE.md → The Landing Page; WCAG 1.3.1, 2.4.6). *Fix:* an `<h2>` above the badge grid (visually hidden if the design cannot carry it) — which also gives zone 2's emphasis a programmatic carrier and resolves §3's first finding.
- **[medium]** **The badge grid has no list semantics.** Seven anchors in a `<div class="grid">` plus one outside it. Visually it is a set of eight peer destinations with a known count and a ruled order; programmatically it is loose links with no boundary and no count (mockups/key-landing-mobile.html; WCAG 1.3.1). *Fix:* `<ul>`/`<li>`, with the emphasised badge as item 1 of the same list — it is item 1 of the ruled badge order, and splitting it out of the list contradicts that order.

- **[low]** The `<h1>` is ruled as "tournament name **+ site name**"; the mock renders "Mundial 2026" only. The `<h1>` and the `<title>` should agree, and this is the arrival surface where they are most likely to be read together (WCAG 2.4.2, 2.4.6).
